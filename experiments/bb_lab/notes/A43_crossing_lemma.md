## §0 Resume here

The tour-de-gross conjecture (arXiv:2506.03094) for the member
(ℓ, m) = (6(r+b), 6r), b ∈ {0, 1}, is

    d(C_{r,b}) = 6(2r + b − 1) = ℓ + m − 6.

Both rows are one formula.  §1 reduces the lower bound to **Theorem W**
(A42: the axis-cylinder floor floor_cyl(p) = 2p) plus **one new torus
lemma, L3** (§2): every nontrivial X-logical with no cyclic x-gap ≥ 4 and
no cyclic y-gap ≥ 4 weighs at least ℓ + m − 6.  The bit b enters the
proof in exactly one place, the final minimum
min(2m, 2ℓ, ℓ + m − 6) = ℓ + m − 6 ⟺ ℓ − m ≤ 6.  Nothing else sees b.

## §1 The reduction

### §1.1 Setting and conventions

Fixed Laurent pair (A40 §1): A = 1 + y + x³y⁻¹, B = 1 + x + x⁻¹y⁻³ on
Z_ℓ × Z_m, two blocks of qubits indexed by the group, H_X = [M_A | M_B],
H_Z = [M_Bᵀ | M_Aᵀ] (`bb_lab.tower.TowerCode`; READ/BND offset tables in
`a40_s11_compare.py`).  An X-logical is v ∈ ker H_Z ∖ rowspace H_X.  The
Z-check at g reads block-0 cells g + {(0,0), (1,0), (−1,−3)} and block-1
cells g + {(0,0), (0,1), (3,−1)}: its x-offsets are {−1, 0, 1, 3} and its
y-offsets {−3, −1, 0, 1}, spread 4 in each axis.

**Cyclic gaps.**  For v with occupied column set X ⊂ Z_ℓ, an x-gap is a
maximal run of consecutive empty columns (cyclically); gap_x(v) is the
longest one; likewise gap_y.  v is **x-windowed** if gap_x ≥ 4,
**y-windowed** if gap_y ≥ 4, and **gap-dense** (doubly spanning) if
gap_x ≤ 3 and gap_y ≤ 3.  Threshold 4 is the check spread: a run of
≥ 4 empty columns cannot lie strictly between two cells read by one
Z-check, so no check straddles such a gap.  This is exactly S11's
`SPAN = 4` and its sector labels (A40 §16.2).

### §1.2 Lemma G (no local logicals) and Lemma K (windowing)

> **Lemma G.**  A nontrivial X-logical of any member has no x-gap ≥ 4
> together with a y-gap ≥ 4.

Proof (banked as Lemma K, A40 §6, "gap dichotomy + Koszul"; sketch).
With both gaps the support sits in a rectangle no check straddles, so v
lifts injectively to a compactly supported plane cycle ṽ.  On the plane
the syzygy module of (A, B) over F₂[x^±, y^±] is generated by the
stabilizer generator because gcd(A, B) = 1 — the variety V(A, B) in
(F̄₂^×)² is finite (four points, dim F₂[x^±,y^±]/(A,B) = 6, A42 Theorem
A), and a nonunit common factor would have a curve of zeros.  So ṽ is a
plane boundary ∂h̃ with h̃ compact, and v = ∂(π_* h̃) is a torus
stabilizer.  ∎

> **Lemma K′ (one-axis windowing move; A40 §6, S11 Lemma U).**  If v is
> nontrivial with gap_x ≥ 4 then v is (0,m)-compact and
> |v| ≥ N((0,m)) = floor_cyl(m).  If gap_y ≥ 4 then |v| ≥ floor_cyl′(ℓ),
> the mirror floor of the period-ℓ cylinder.

Proof.  Cut the torus along the gap: the support lifts injectively to
the cylinder Z × Z_m and, since no check straddles the gap, the lift is
a cylinder cycle; a compact trivializer of the lift would push forward
to a trivializer of v (Lemma U), so the lift is nontrivial and its
weight is ≥ the cylinder floor.  ∎

### §1.3 Theorem W (A42)

> **Theorem W (A42 §2.16, §2.17.7).**  floor_cyl(p) = 2p for every
> p ∈ 6ℤ, p ≥ 6.  Tiers: certificate at p ≤ 12 (full), pure sector to
> p ≤ 21; for general p modulo (R1) the pure σ-floor and (R2) the
> hiding-mass inequality (HM) at q = p/3, which A42 §2.17.4 packages as
> one seam-doubling statement; when 9 | p the Z₉-fibre form (HM₉) of
> A42 §2.17.5 replaces (HM).

By the x↔y, A↔B mirror the same theorem gives floor_cyl′(ℓ) = 2ℓ.

### §1.4 Lemma A (assembly) and where b enters

> **Lemma A.**  Assume Theorem W at p = m and p = ℓ, and L3 at (ℓ, m).
> Then every nontrivial X-logical of (ℓ, m) weighs
> ≥ min(2m, 2ℓ, ℓ + m − 6), and for ℓ ≥ m this minimum equals
> ℓ + m − 6 if and only if ℓ − m ≤ 6.  With U1 (b = 1) and U0 (b = 0):
> d(C_{r,b}) = ℓ + m − 6 = 6(2r + b − 1) for b ∈ {0, 1}, all r.

Proof.  Trichotomy by §1.1: gap_x ≥ 4 gives 2m (Lemma K′ + W),
gap_y ≥ 4 gives 2ℓ, gap-dense gives ℓ + m − 6 (L3); Lemma G is the
empty fourth case.  2m ≤ ℓ + m − 6 ⟺ ℓ − m ≥ 6 and 2ℓ ≥ ℓ + m − 6
always; so the minimum is ℓ + m − 6 exactly when ℓ − m ≤ 6, i.e.
b ∈ {0, 1}, with the tie 2m = ℓ + m − 6 at b = 1.  ∎

Three remarks.
1. **Only the shorter period needs the exact floor.**  At b = 1 the
   x-wrapping case has slack 12 (2ℓ = 2m + 12 against 2m needed) and at
   b = 0 both wrapping cases have slack 6.  Theorem W is needed sharp
   only at p = m = 6r, b = 1; a weaker floor_cyl(p) ≥ 2p − 6 suffices
   everywhere else.
2. **The b = 0 "violations" are not violations.**  At (6,6) and
   (12,12) the toroidal minima 6 and 18 sit below the windowed floors
   12 and 24 (A40 §16.2); in this reduction that is L3's bound beating
   Theorem W's, exactly as Lemma A predicts for ℓ − m < 6.  The
   comparison theorem of A40 §16 (min_D ≥ min_W at b = 1) is the
   specialization of L3 to ℓ = m + 6 and is no longer a separate
   target; its surgery program is superseded.
3. **The registry note that the formula fails at b = 2** (A40 S1,
   d((18,6)) = 12) is Lemma A's ℓ − m > 6 case: the windowed 2m wins.

## §2 Lemma L3 — the crossing lemma

### §2.1 Statement

> **L3 (crossing lemma; conjecture, target of A43-S2).**  For every
> frame (ℓ, m) ∈ 6ℤ × 6ℤ with ℓ, m ≥ 6 (no restriction on b, on which
> of ℓ, m is larger, or on 9 | ℓm), every nontrivial gap-dense
> X-logical v satisfies |v| ≥ ℓ + m − 6.

Sector form (weaker, sufficient for Lemma A): min_D(ℓ, m) ≥ ℓ + m − 6
where D is S11's toroidal sector (A40 §16.0: no injective compact lift
to either axis cylinder).  D ⊆ {gap-dense} by Lemma K′, and gap-dense
objects outside D are covered by Theorem W directly, so the two forms
are interchangeable for the assembly; the gap-dense form is the one
whose hypothesis is local and checkable.

L3 subsumes, in one b-blind statement: S11's (H1) twisted-cylinder
floors for non-axis directions, its (H2) mixed-sector floor, and its
Prop E surgery for 2D representatives of rank-one classes.

### §2.2 Evidence and the constant

|---|---|---|---|---|
| (6,6) | 0 | 6 | 6 (84 objects, all D) | A40 §16.2 census, re-derived §5 |
| (12,12) | 0 | 18 | 18 (a36 witness + 48 shear pullbacks) | A40 §16.2 |

| (24,18) | 1 | 36 | ≥ 12 (A40 §15 floor) | open |

So the constant 6 is forced by the three small frames and is not
attained at (18,12).  An equality law min_D = ℓ + m − 6 for all
frames is **refuted** at (18,12) (ledger §4); only the lower bound is
conjectured.

### §2.3 Decomposition (working plan for A43-S2; the data of §5 rule
### out the naive form)

- **L3a (dirty cuts).**  For a vertical cut between columns j and j+1
  the *cut state* s_j is the vector of left-partial parities of the
  Z-checks whose read-window straddles the cut (check columns j−2..j+1,
  four per row).  s_j = 0 (a *clean* cut) makes v (0,m)-compact via the
  Lemma K′ lift, hence |v| ≥ 2m ≥ ℓ + m − 6 whenever ℓ − m ≤ 6.  So for
  the b ∈ {0,1} rows L3 may assume **every axis cut is dirty**; for
  the b-blind statement the clean-cut case needs 2m ≥ ℓ + m − 6, i.e.
  it is exactly the ℓ − m ≤ 6 rows again — L3 in full generality must
  handle clean cuts on its own, but Lemma A never needs that.
- **L3b (tension).**  Keeping every vertical cut dirty costs
  ≥ ℓ − c_x qubits; rows likewise with c_y.  One proof serves both by
  the mirror.  The naive support-level version ("one occupied column
  per cut") is FALSE: §5.2 shows gross minimizers with only 4 of 12
  columns occupied.  The dirty-cut hypothesis is syndrome-level: a
  single column keeps up to four cuts dirty through the offset-3 read
  (x³y⁻¹ in A), so the accounting must be a potential on cut states,
  not a count of columns.
- **L3c (corner).**  The excess ℓ + m − |v| is ≤ 6.  In the
  full-occupancy family (§5.1: 12 objects at (6,6), 48 at (12,12), 12
  at gross) exactly 6 qubits serve both a column and a row; the other
  families realize the same total by different local means (§5.2), so
  "6 double payers" is a description of one family, not the mechanism.

## §3 The covering no-go (theorem)

> **Proposition (cap on dual-covering bounds).**  For a nontrivial
> X-class c let LP(c) be the value of the fractional packing LP
> max Σ_z λ_z over Z-logicals z with ⟨c, z⟩ = 1, λ ≥ 0, subject to
> load Σ_{z ∋ q} λ_z ≤ 1 at every qubit q.  Then LP(c) ≤ n / d_Z, and
> any lower bound on |v| obtained by counting the qubits of v against
> a family of anticommuting Z-logicals (disjoint translates, fractional
> packings, LP duals) is at most LP(c).

Proof.  Σ_q load(q) = Σ_z λ_z |z| ≥ d_Z Σ_z λ_z and Σ_q load(q) ≤ n.  ∎

For the members n = 2ℓm and d_Z = d (transpose duality), so the cap is
2ℓm/(ℓ+m−6): at b = 1 it is 6(r+1) = ℓ, at b = 0 it is 6r²/(2r−1) < ℓ + 3.
The toric code saturates this cap (its distance equals its LP), the
members do not: a covering argument can prove at most d ≥ ℓ + 3, half
the truth.  Consequently every route to ℓ + m − 6 must charge some
qubits twice — per-row exact accounting (A42's Fibre Theorem, whose
weight identity is not a covering), or a syndrome-level crossing
argument (L3b).  The momentum-budget floor 3.625r of A40 §15 is below
the cap; the cap is not what stopped it, but it does say no
refinement of covering-type discharging can reach 12r.

### §5.2 What the data fix

1. **Every D-minimizer has every axis cut dirty** (dirty_x = ℓ,
   dirty_y = m for all 1,225 objects), as L3a predicts; cut-state
   weights range 1–6 (vertical) and 2–8 (horizontal).  The W_x control
   has 2–5 dirty vertical cuts and clean ones elsewhere.
2. **Support-level counting fails.**  Occupied columns n_x at gross
   range over {4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 12} and rows n_y over {2, 4, 5, 6};
   x-gaps of 3 (one below the windowing threshold) occur in 360 of the
   1,092 D-minimizers.  The identity ℓ + m − |v| = (empty columns) +
   (empty rows) + (n_x + n_y − |v|) = 6 is split differently by each
   family: helix (0, 0, 6), x-band (2, 4, 0), y-band (8, 1, −3), blobs
   (4–5, 0–1, −1…1).  No fixed per-axis constants exist: an x-band
   pays ℓ on columns and m − 6 on rows, a y-band the mirror, the
   helix ℓ + m with 6 shared.  L3 must be proved as the **joint**
   bound; the split L3b/L3c of §2.3 is a description of the helix
   family only.
3. **Rank-one classes reach the D-floor.**  All three gross
   non-blob families lie in pure-y (x-wrapping) classes and cost
   ℓ + m − 6 = 12 = 2ℓ − 12, twelve below their compact floor.  No
   pure-x (y-wrapping) class has a D-representative at 12: the 2m
   floor is not undercut by 2D representatives at gross.  The mixed
   classes are realized only by blobs at gross.
4. **The families scale differently.**  x-bands cost ℓ (2 rows) at
   m = 6 and 3ℓ/2 (4 rows) at m = 12, helices ℓ + m − 6 where they
   exist (square frames and ℓ = 2m), and at (18,12) none of the
   families reaches 24 (A42 §2.17.2: D-sector ≥ 26).  A proof of L3
   cannot be family-specific; a description of min_D(ℓ, m) beyond
   the lower bound is open (A44).
5. **Block-pure objects are period-3 structures**: bands live in one
   block on rows (columns) spaced by 3, the y³ (x³) coupling of B (A);
   in A42's fibre language they are singleton-fibre objects (weight 1
   per Z₃-fibre, all fibres in S ∩ s), the cheapest fibre pattern,
   closed through the torus's second direction — exactly the pattern
   (HM) forbids on a cylinder.  This is the bridge between L3 and the
   A42 toolbox.
