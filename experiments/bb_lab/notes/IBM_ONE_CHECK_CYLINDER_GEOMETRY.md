## 0. Result and exact scope

This historical partial argument does not itself prove the unrestricted
cylinder floor `d_cyl(6r)>=4r`; the linked completed proof does.
The following physical lower bounds and obstructions are proved here
for every positive ell divisible by six:

1. A genuinely weight-contracting diagonal projection detects 48 of
   the 63 nonzero binary cylinder classes. Every representative of
   each detected class has weight at least `2ell/3`. The minimum
   over this collection of classes is exactly `2ell/3`.
2. Every nonzero g-only cylinder cycle has weight at least `2ell/3`.
   If its diagonal projection is zero, its weight is at least
   `4ell/3`, attained by a nontrivial class.
3. Every nonzero cylinder cycle occupying at most two y rows has
   weight at least `2ell/3`. If its first block is nonzero, its
   weight is at least `4ell/3`; this latter value is attained.
4. The projection misses exactly the four-dimensional doubled
   primary component, containing fifteen nonzero classes. Explicit
   nontrivial families in its kernel witness both the nilpotent
   and unit parts. No monomial substitution `y=x^s` matches that
   whole doubled component. The tempting substitution `y=x+1`
   also has an explicit unbounded physical-weight amplification,
   even on chains whose every first-block row is divisible by chi.

At the time of this partial pass, these results restricted any possible
counterexample to the fifteen purely doubled classes, with f nonzero
and at least three actual y rows. This was not a width-completeness
assertion or a full exact-distance proof. The subsequent seam argument
excludes those possible counterexamples without making this projection
injective or repairing the noncontracting substitutions below.

## 1. Physical coordinates and the banked algebra

Let

```
F=x^ell+1,        A_ell=F2[x,x^-1]/(F),
chi=1+x+x^2,     U=y+x,       V=y+x+1,
P=x^3+y+y^2,     Q=chi V,
D(f,g)=Pf+Qg,    boundary(h)=(Qh,Ph).
```

Cylinder chains belong to `A_ell[y,y^-1]^2`, with finite support
in the unbounded y coordinate. All weights count nonzero physical
binary coefficients; a change of module basis is not assigned zero
physical cost.

The independently reviewed one-check notes prove regularity of P,Q
over the plane Laurent ring, that F annihilates their plane module
M, and the following exact primary decomposition:

```
H_cyl(ell)=M,
M=M_simple direct-product M_double,
M_simple=R/(chi,U)=F4,
M_double=R/(chi^2,V+x chi)=F4[epsilon]/epsilon^2.     (1.1)
```

In the double component, write

```
theta^2+theta+1=0, epsilon^2=0,
x=theta+epsilon,    y=theta^2(1+epsilon),
chi=epsilon,       V=x chi,       U=1+V.            (1.2)
```

For a finite plane lift of a cylinder cycle, its syndrome is
`F A(x,y)`. Its cylinder homology class is `[A] in M`. These are
the homology conventions used below; no torus quotient in the y
direction is introduced.

### 1.1 Row divisibility and two elementary cyclic distances

Every physical row of f is divisible by chi. Indeed, evaluation at
x=omega sends the cycle equation to

```
(1+y+y^2) f(omega,y)=0
```

in the domain `F4[y,y^-1]`. Thus f(omega,y)=0, row by row. Because
3 divides ell, this evaluation is well-defined and its binary
kernel in A_ell is exactly the ideal generated by chi. In particular
an occupied f row costs at least two sites.

We will also use exact elementary recurrence bounds:

```
0!=a in ker(chi:A_ell->A_ell)   implies |a|>=2ell/3;
0!=a in ker(chi^2:A_ell->A_ell) implies |a|>=ell/3.  (1.3)
```

For the first, the coefficients obey the recurrence
`a_j+a_(j-1)+a_(j-2)=0`. A nonzero solution repeats one of
110,101,011, hence has exactly `2ell/3` sites. For the second,
`chi^2=1+x^2+x^4`; the two parity subwords independently satisfy
the first recurrence at length ell/2, a multiple of three. Every
nonzero parity subword has `ell/3` sites. This proves (1.3) for
all ell in scope, not merely square-free periods.

## 2. A weight-contracting diagonal detector

Define the physical linear map

```
Psi(f,g)=x f(x,x)+g(x,x) in A_ell.                  (2.1)
```

Here y is replaced by the monomial x. This is well-defined even for
negative y exponents, since x is a unit. Each input physical site
maps to one output site, and parity summation only cancels sites.
Consequently

```
|Psi(f,g)| <= |f|+|g|.                              (2.2)
```

The exact diagonal check identities are

```
P(x,x)=x chi,       Q(x,x)=chi.                     (2.3)
```

Thus any cylinder cycle has `chi Psi(f,g)=0`. Any boundary has
Psi zero, since `x Q(x,x)+P(x,x)=0`.

**Theorem 2.1.** A cylinder cycle with nonzero Psi is nontrivial
and has physical weight at least `2ell/3`.

This follows immediately from (1.3) and (2.2). In particular this
is a physical certificate, not merely an injective homology map.

### 2.1 Exactly which classes the detector sees

Put `W=F/chi`. Choose plane lifts f_tilde,g_tilde of a cylinder
cycle and write `P f_tilde+Q g_tilde=F A`. Evaluation at y=x gives

```
chi [x f_tilde(x,x)+g_tilde(x,x)]=chi W A(x,x).
```

The unbounded one-variable Laurent ring is a domain, so cancel chi
before reducing modulo F. Therefore

```
Psi(f,g)=W A(x,x) modulo F.                         (2.4)
```

Multiplication by W identifies `F2[x,x^-1]/chi` with ker chi in
A_ell: `W a=0 mod F` if and only if chi divides a. Hence Psi is
nonzero exactly when `[A]` has nonzero residue in M_simple.
Its kernel on homology is precisely `{0} times M_double`.

There are three nonzero simple residues and sixteen doubled
residues. Thus Theorem 2.1 covers exactly 48 nonzero classes,
including arbitrary doubled components accompanying a nonzero
simple residue. Fifteen nonzero purely doubled classes remain.

The one-row stripe `(0,W)` has Psi=W and weight `2ell/3`, so the
minimum over the detected classes is exactly this value. This does
not claim that every one of the 48 individual classes has a stripe
attainer.

## 3. Additional all-class bounds with physical support hypotheses

### 3.1 All g-only cycles, without a support-height restriction

If f=0, the cycle equation is `V(chi g)=0`. Multiplication by
`V=y+x+1` is injective on finite Laurent y polynomials over A_ell:
its highest y term has coefficient one. Therefore chi g=0.
Every occupied g row has exactly `2ell/3` sites by (1.3).

This proves the `2ell/3` floor for every nonzero g-only cycle,
including all classes it can represent. If only one g row is
occupied, evaluation y=x multiplies that nonzero row by a unit
monomial, so Psi is nonzero. Thus a nonzero g-only cycle with
Psi=0 has at least two occupied rows and weight at least `4ell/3`.
Section 4 supplies a nontrivial attainer for this restricted bound.

### 3.2 All cycles occupying at most two rows

First suppose the two possible rows are adjacent. Translate them
to 0 and 1, writing `f=f0+y f1`, `g=g0+y g1`. The coefficient
of y^3 in D(f,g) forces f1=0. The remaining equations are

```
f0=chi g1,
chi(g0+x g1)=0,
chi[x^3 g1+(x+1)g0]=0.                             (3.1)
```

Put `k=g0+x g1`, so chi k=0. Substituting in the final equation
gives `x chi^2 g1=0`. Since x is a unit, chi^2 g1=0. Consequently

```
chi f0=0,        chi^2 g1=0,        chi^2 g0=0.     (3.2)
```

If f0 is nonzero, both g1 and g0 are nonzero: g1=0 would force
f0=0, and g0=0 would make chi g1=0 by the middle equation of
(3.1). Applying (1.3) to all three nonzero masks yields

```
|f|+|g| >= 2ell/3+ell/3+ell/3=4ell/3.              (3.3)
```

If f0=0, section 3.1 applies instead and gives `2ell/3` for every
nonzero cycle.

For two occupied rows separated by d>=2, translate them to 0,d.
The top syndrome coefficients first give f_d=0 and chi g_d=0.
If d>2, the y^2 coefficient then gives f0=0. If d=2, that
coefficient is `f0+(x+1)chi g_d=f0`, with the same conclusion.
Thus every such cycle is g-only. A single occupied row is covered
by the same highest-row argument.

This proves the floor for **all** cycles occupying at most two
actual rows, not only one chosen homology class or two-row period.
No y folding is used; highest-row coefficients survive because
y is unbounded.

For sharpness of (3.3), define

```
T=F/chi^2=(1+x^2) sum_(j=0)^(ell/6-1) x^(6j),
W=chi T=(1+x+x^3+x^4) sum_(j=0)^(ell/6-1) x^(6j).
```

They have weights `ell/3` and `2ell/3`, respectively. The exact
plane identity

```
chi P+U Q=x chi^2
```

gives the cylinder cycle

```
z0=(x^-1 W, x^-1 U T),       D z0=F.               (3.4)
```

It has one nonzero f row and two g rows, and weight `4ell/3`.
Its period coordinate is 1 in M, so it is nontrivial. Thus (3.3)
is also attained in a nontrivial class, for every ell in scope.

## 4. Explicit nontrivial classes invisible to Psi

The blindness of the diagonal projection cannot be removed by
claiming that every nontrivial class has a nonzero simple residue.
There are explicit physical counterexamples to that claim.

First take

```
z_nil=(0,U W),       D z_nil=F U V.                 (4.1)
```

Its simple period residue is zero. In the double component,
`UV=(1+V)V=V=x chi` is a nonzero nilpotent. It is therefore a
nontrivial cylinder cycle. The two g rows are disjoint, so
`|z_nil|=4ell/3`; also Psi is exactly zero since U(x,x)=0.
This attains the g-only zero-detector bound in section 3.1.

There is also an invisible class which is a unit in the double
component, rather than merely nilpotent. Multiply (3.4) by U:

```
z_double=(x^-1 U W, x^-1 U^2 T),
D z_double=F U,       |z_double|=2ell.              (4.2)
```

The first block has two W rows, totaling `4ell/3`, and the second
has two T rows because `U^2=y^2+x^2`, totaling `2ell/3`.
The class U is zero in the simple component and equals the unit
1+V in the double component. Thus it is nontrivial and Psi=0.

Moreover UM is exactly the doubled summand: U vanishes in the
simple factor and is a unit in the double factor. Consequently
the R-module span of this invisible class is the entire kernel
of Psi on homology. No weight bound on those module combinations
is asserted.

These are obstructions to completeness of this particular detector,
not counterexamples to the proposed cylinder distance: both displayed
weights are above `2ell/3`.

## 5. Why two tempting replacements are not metric certificates

### 5.1 No single monomial slope realizes the doubled tangent

In M_double, (1.2) gives x^6=1. If a monomial substitution y=x^s
agreed with the whole doubled component, reduction modulo epsilon
would require `s=2 mod 3`. There are only two possibilities modulo
six:

```
x^2=theta^2,
x^5=theta^2+theta epsilon.
```

Neither equals `y=theta^2+theta^2 epsilon`. Thus no integer s gives
the desired identity on this length-two component. This rules out
simply replacing the successful monomial slope y=x by a different
single binary monomial slope and asserting that it realizes the
full doubled component. It does not exclude more elaborate geometric
projections or cost-controlled multi-output constructions.

### 5.2 The reduced-point substitution can amplify weight without bound

The substitution y=x+1 only specifies the reduced double point,
not its tangent `V=x chi`. It is not even a homomorphism on the
whole Laurent cylinder ring: x+1 is a zero divisor in A_ell and
cannot be the image of the unit y.

Even restricting to nonnegative y powers does not give a physical
contraction. For any integer a>=2, choose

```
ell=6*2^a,       j=2^a-2,
v_a=((1+x^3)y^j,0).
```

This chain has weight two, and its occupied f row is divisible
by chi. But

```
f_a(x,x+1)
  =(1+x^3)(1+x)^j
  =(1+x^3) sum_(k=0)^(2^(a-1)-1) x^(2k).           (5.1)
```

The two summands occupy opposite x parities and do not overlap;
their largest exponent is `2^a+1<ell`, so no period folding occurs.
The output weight is exactly `2^a`. The amplification from weight
two is therefore unbounded, despite the row-divisibility condition.

These inputs are not asserted to be cycles. The example rules out
inferring contraction from the row-divisibility hypothesis alone;
it is not a distance counterexample or an impossibility theorem
for estimates which exploit the complete cycle equation.

## 6. Historical residual and subsequent closure

For ell=6r, the results above prove the target `4r` floor on all
48 detected classes, all g-only cycles, and all cycles occupying
at most two rows. At this partial stage, any possible vector below
the target had to satisfy all of the following:

```
Pf+Qg=0,       Psi(f,g)=0,       f!=0,
at least three occupied y rows,
0!=[f,g] in {0} times M_double.
```

The former residual statement was that every such nontrivial cycle
has weight at least `2ell/3`. Neither a bound on its y span nor
a replacement by a bounded-row representative is proved here.
These partial certificates alone did not improve the then-banked
interval `2r<=d_cyl(6r)<=4r` for the full code.

The parent reduction proves `d_tor(6r,6r)=d_cyl(6r)`. The subsequent
all-class cylinder proof in `IBM_ONE_CHECK_CYLINDER_TANGENT.md` closes
the residual and gives `d_cyl(6r)=d_tor(6r,6r)=4r`; the full theorem
is in `IBM_MODIFIED_EXACT_DISTANCE_THEOREM.md`. Nothing here or in
that modified-family theorem proves the distance conjecture for the
unchanged original IBM check polynomial.

ell=12 instance of the stripe and the three explicit class witnesses,
